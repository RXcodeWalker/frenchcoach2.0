Français AI Design System · v2 · Oct 2026

Parle, et on t’écoute. A system for speaking practice.

One system for every screen of the speaking coach. Flat surfaces separated by hairlines, colour chosen by role, motion that confirms rather than decorates. Every value below maps 1:1 to the token names in src/index.css, so adopting it is a token swap.

01One primary per screenViolet fill means “do this next”. Nothing else gets it.
02Colour by role, never varietyProgress, reward, streak, correction, info. Topics are the only decorative hue.
03Hierarchy from surface + hairlineNo glow, no gradient, no blur. Shadows only on things that float.
04Reward is earned, onceCelebration plays on the event, then stops. Nothing loops or pulses.

01 — Colour

A warm neutral ground, five roles, one action.

Ratios are computed live from the values shown. Text tokens report their worst case across --surface, --surface-recessed and --bg; fills report against --surface at the 3:1 non-text threshold; soft tints report their paired text token on top.

Why violet stays

It is the only hue on the wheel not already claimed by a role: green is progress, amber is reward, orange is streak, red is correction, cyan is info. Violet can mean “act” without being confused for any of them. It is already the logo, the rail and every existing CTA, and it is unclaimed by the big language apps students already use. White on #7C3AED is 5.7:1, so the fill holds AA at 13px.

What changed

The cool slate body (#f1f5f9) hard-coded in body fought the warm token layer. The ground is now one warm neutral via --bg. Light reward, streak and progress move one step darker so their text holds AA on recessed surfaces too. Dark hover now lightens instead of failing contrast.

02 — Type

Playful by default. Formal when it counts.

Bricolage Grotesque carries the whole app. Its optical-size axis makes headlines chunky and friendly while body text stays open, so one family does the work of two. Body runs Light so screens breathe; weight is spent on things you read first or act on. Libre Baskerville appears only when the stakes are formal — Exam mode and quoted model answers — so the switch itself tells a student this is the real thing.

05 — Motion

Eight motions, choreographed.

Each motion has one job. Sequences run container → content → number, so the eye is led rather than distracted. Springs give the tactile moments weight; tweens keep data honest. Replaces motion/variants.ts. With useReducedMotion() every motion keeps its opacity and drops travel, scale and sparks.

07 — Do / don’t

The rules that stop the drift.

Do
  1. One primary button per screen. Everything else is secondary or quiet.
  2. Pick colour by role: progress, reward, streak, correction, info. Topic hue only inside its icon chip.
  3. Bricolage everywhere; body at Light 300, never below Regular under 14px. Libre Baskerville only in Exam mode and quoted model answers.
  4. Use role tokens (text-ink-muted, bg-surface) so light and dark come from the same class.
  5. Animate with the eight named motions and nothing else. Choreograph: container first, then content, then the number.
  6. Design the empty and unscored states before the happy path.
Don’t
  1. No emoji anywhere in the UI — topics, streaks, buttons, toasts, avatars.
  2. No gradients, glows or blur on controls or cards. Delete .btn-primary and pulseGlow.
  3. No scale on hover, no 3D tilt, no looping pulse or ping. Press may compress to 0.97; that is the only scale on a control.
  4. No text under 12px, no weight above 700, no uppercase on French, no third typeface.
  5. No text-white patched in light mode, no raw violet-500/10, no hex in components.
  6. No opacity to signal disabled or locked — use the recessed and ink-subtle tokens.
